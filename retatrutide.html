Protocol & Stability

Protocol & Expiry Tracking

Monitoring vial longevity, dosing schedule, and the 28–30 day discard rule.

When working with reconstituted peptides, maintaining an exact timeline is essential. Once bacteriostatic water (containing 0.9% benzyl alcohol) is introduced to the vial, laboratory standards generally establish an in-use shelf life of 28 to 30 days when stored continuously under refrigeration at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C).

By entering your intended dosing frequency and mixing date into the calculator's optional tracking panel, the system computes two critical operational figures:

  • Vial Lifespan (Days): Calculated as total doses contained in the vial divided by doses administered per day. This indicates whether the vial volume will be consumed before chemical stability degrades.
  • Discard Date: Projected exactly 28 days following the reconstitution date. If your protocol schedule exceeds 28–30 days, the calculator flags an extended vial duration warning.
Mathematics

Retatrutide Reconstitution & Calculation Math

Formulas and mechanics governing liquid dilution and syringe calibration.

Retatrutide is manufactured as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder sealed in sterile glass vials under vacuum. Because the powder has negligible displacement volume once dissolved, the liquid volume of the reconstituted solution is equal to the volume of bacteriostatic water added.

The mathematical relationship governing every draw consists of three sequential formulas:

  1. Concentration (mg/mL): Total peptide mass in milligrams divided by the added bacteriostatic water volume in milliliters. For example: 10 mg ÷ 2 mL = 5 mg/mL (5,000 mcg/mL).
  2. Draw Volume (mL): Target dose divided by solution concentration. If the target dose is 2,000 mcg (2 mg), then: 2 mg ÷ 5 mg/mL = 0.40 mL.
  3. Syringe Calibration (Units): Draw volume in milliliters multiplied by syringe calibration factor. On a standard U-100 syringe (100 units/mL): 0.40 mL × 100 = 40 units.

This process is purely mathematical. No health or therapeutic claims are made, and users must refer to professional medical directives for protocol specifications.

Vial Specifications

Common Retatrutide Vial Sizes

Comparing 5 mg, 10 mg, and 20 mg vials and dilution mechanics.

Retatrutide research vials are commonly supplied in three standard quantities: 5 mg, 10 mg, and 20 mg. The total milligram content defines the total active substance available, but does not dictate a single fixed volume.

  • 5 mg Vials: Typically reconstituted with 1 mL or 2 mL of bacteriostatic water, yielding concentrations of 5.0 mg/mL (50 mcg/unit) or 2.5 mg/mL (25 mcg/unit) respectively on a standard U-100 syringe.
  • 10 mg Vials: Commonly reconstituted with 2 mL or 3 mL of bacteriostatic water, yielding concentrations of 5.0 mg/mL (50 mcg/unit) or 3.33 mg/mL (33.3 mcg/unit).
  • 20 mg Vials: Often reconstituted with 2 mL, 3 mL, or 5 mL of bacteriostatic water, resulting in concentrations of 10.0 mg/mL (100 mcg/unit), 6.67 mg/mL (66.7 mcg/unit), or 4.0 mg/mL (40 mcg/unit).

Selecting the reconstitution volume is an operational balance: adding more BAC water decreases solution concentration, producing a larger draw volume with more syringe markings for higher visual measuring precision; adding less BAC water produces a concentrated solution requiring fewer units to draw.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Retatrutide reconstitution math, measurements, and stability facts.

How many units is 2 mg of Retatrutide?

In a 10 mg vial mixed with 2 mL BAC water the concentration is 5 mg/mL, so 2 mg (2000 mcg) = 0.40 mL = 40 units on a standard U-100 syringe (0.40 mL × 100 units/mL = 40 units).

The exact unit count changes depending on how the vial is mixed. For instance, if a 5 mg vial is reconstituted with 2 mL BAC water, the concentration is 2.5 mg/mL, and a 2 mg dose would require 0.80 mL (80 units). Use the calculator above to check the exact units for your specific vial size and water volume.

Is 1 mg the same as 1000 mcg?

Yes: 1 mg = 1000 mcg (pure metric conversion). The calculator asks for the dose in mcg by default, but also includes a quick mcg/mg toggle. To convert milligrams to micrograms, multiply by 1,000 (e.g. 1 mg × 1,000 = 1,000 mcg). To convert micrograms to milligrams, divide by 1,000 (e.g. 1,000 mcg ÷ 1,000 = 1 mg).

How many doses are in a 10 mg vial?

The total doses in a vial is calculated by dividing total vial milligrams by the dose amount in milligrams. For example, at 2 mg per dose, a 10 mg vial holds exactly 5 doses (10 mg ÷ 2 mg = 5 doses). If the dose is 1 mg (1,000 mcg), the vial holds 10 doses (10 mg ÷ 1 mg = 10 doses). This is a purely mathematical relationship based on vial content.

How long can a mixed vial be used?

Once reconstituted with bacteriostatic water, a mixed vial is generally used within 28–30 days when stored continuously refrigerated at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C). The calculator shows your projected vial days and discard date using this 28–30 day guidance in the optional protocol tracking section.
