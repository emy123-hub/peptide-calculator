Protocol & Stability

Protocol & Expiry Tracking

Monitoring vial longevity, dosing schedule, and the 28–30 day discard rule.

When working with reconstituted peptides, maintaining an exact timeline is essential. Once bacteriostatic water (containing 0.9% benzyl alcohol) is introduced to the vial, laboratory standards generally establish an in-use shelf life of 28 to 30 days when stored continuously under refrigeration at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C).

By entering your intended dosing frequency and mixing date into the calculator's optional tracking panel, the system computes two critical operational figures:

  • Vial Lifespan (Days): Calculated as total doses contained in the vial divided by doses administered per day. This indicates whether the vial volume will be consumed before chemical stability degrades.
  • Discard Date: Projected exactly 28 days following the reconstitution date. If your protocol schedule exceeds 28–30 days, the calculator flags an extended vial duration warning.
Mathematics

GHK-Cu Reconstitution & Calculation Math

Formulas and mechanics governing liquid dilution and syringe calibration.

GHK-Cu is manufactured as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder sealed in sterile glass vials under vacuum. A mixed GHK-Cu solution normally has a blue tint because the peptide contains copper. Because the lyophilized powder occupies negligible volume when dissolved, the final liquid volume of the reconstituted solution is equal to the volume of bacteriostatic water added.

The mathematical relationship governing every draw consists of three sequential formulas:

  1. Concentration (mg/mL): Total peptide mass in milligrams divided by the added bacteriostatic water volume in milliliters. For example: 50 mg ÷ 5 mL = 10 mg/mL (10,000 mcg/mL).
  2. Draw Volume (mL): Target dose in milligrams divided by solution concentration in mg/mL. For example, if the target dose is 1,000 mcg (1 mg): 1 mg ÷ 10 mg/mL = 0.10 mL.
  3. Syringe Calibration (Units): Draw volume in milliliters multiplied by syringe calibration factor. On a standard U-100 syringe (100 units/mL): 0.10 mL × 100 = 10 units.

This process is purely mathematical. No health, skin, hair, healing, or therapeutic claims are made, and users must refer to licensed healthcare directives for protocol specifications.

Vial Specifications

Common GHK-Cu Vial Sizes

Comparing 50 mg and 100 mg vials and dilution mechanics.

GHK-Cu powder vials are commonly supplied in two standard research sizes: 50 mg and 100 mg. The total milligram content defines the total active substance available in the vial, and the volume of bacteriostatic water added establishes the final liquid concentration.

  • 50 mg in 5 mL BAC water: Solution concentration is 10 mg/mL (100 mcg per U-100 unit).
  • 50 mg in 2 mL BAC water: Solution concentration is 25 mg/mL (250 mcg per U-100 unit).
  • 100 mg in 5 mL BAC water: Solution concentration is 20 mg/mL (200 mcg per U-100 unit).
  • 100 mg in 2 mL BAC water: Solution concentration is 50 mg/mL (500 mcg per U-100 unit).

Selecting a reconstitution volume involves an operational balance: adding more BAC water (e.g. 5 mL) dilutes the solution, providing more syringe tick marks per dose for higher visual measuring precision, whereas using less BAC water (e.g. 2 mL) creates a more concentrated solution requiring smaller injection volumes.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

GHK-Cu reconstitution math, measurements, and stability facts.

How many units is 1000 mcg (1 mg) of GHK-Cu?

In a 50 mg vial mixed with 5 mL BAC water the concentration is 10 mg/mL, so 1000 mcg (1 mg) = 0.10 mL = 10 units on a standard U-100 syringe (0.10 mL × 100 units/mL = 10 units).

This number changes with how the vial is mixed. For example, if a 50 mg vial is mixed with 2 mL BAC water, the concentration is 25 mg/mL, and 1000 mcg (1 mg) requires 0.04 mL (4 units). Use the calculator above to check the exact units for your specific vial size and water volume.

Why is the mixed GHK-Cu solution blue?

GHK-Cu is a copper peptide, and the blue tint comes from the copper in the solution; the colour is normal for this peptide.

Is 2000 mcg the same as 2 mg?

Yes: 1000 mcg = 1 mg, so 2000 mcg = 2 mg (pure metric conversion). The calculator allows you to enter your dose in either mcg or mg using the unit toggle button.

How long can a mixed vial be used?

Once reconstituted with bacteriostatic water, a mixed vial is generally used within 28–30 days when stored continuously refrigerated at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C). The calculator shows your projected vial days and discard date using this 28–30 day guidance in the optional protocol tracking section.
