Section 4

Why the Same Dose Gives Different Units

Understanding liquid concentration versus active substance mass.

Units are not a dose — they are a volume. A syringe unit is simply a physical tick mark measuring liquid volume (on a standard U-100 syringe, 1 unit equals exactly 0.01 mL). How much active peptide is contained inside that 0.01 mL depends completely on how concentrated the dissolved solution is.

The same 2.5 mg example is 50 units from a 10 mg vial mixed with 2 mL, but only 25 units from a 20 mg vial mixed with 2 mL, because the second mix is twice as strong. In the 10 mg vial with 2 mL of water, the concentration is 5 mg/mL (50 mcg per unit). In the 20 mg vial with 2 mL of water, the concentration doubles to 10 mg/mL (100 mcg per unit). Because each unit holds twice as much peptide, you need only half as many units to deliver the same 2.5 mg amount.

This is why copying someone else's units is the most common maths mistake with tirzepatide. If someone mentions drawing "25 units" or "50 units," that number is entirely meaningless without knowing both the vial size in milligrams and the exact volume of bacteriostatic water added. Always calculate your units based on your own vial's mixing ratio.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about tirzepatide conversions and units.

How many units is 2.5 mg of tirzepatide?

It depends on the mix. From a 10 mg vial with 2 mL of water it is 50 units; from a 20 mg vial with 2 mL it is 25 units. Find your mix in Chart 1, or use the calculator.

What if my dose works out to more than 100 units?

Then it does not fit a standard 1 mL U-100 syringe in one draw. The calculator on this site warns you about this automatically — a weaker mix (more water) brings the units down.

How do I convert mg to mcg?

Multiply by 1,000. 1 mg = 1,000 mcg, 2.5 mg = 2,500 mcg, 5 mg = 5,000 mcg.

Is this chart a dosing schedule?

No. This chart only converts numbers. It does not say how much anyone should take or when — that is never this site's job.
