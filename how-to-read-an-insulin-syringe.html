How to Read an Insulin Syringe: U-100, U-50 and U-40 Explained

Understanding barrel markings, volume scales, and converting units to milliliters with precision.

The number printed on a syringe — U-100, U-50 or U-40 — quietly changes what every line on it means, and using the wrong type's maths is one of the biggest conversion errors beginners make. This page explains the three scales with plain maths, which is exactly why the peptide calculator on this site asks for the syringe type before it answers.

Important Safety Rule: This guide explains only how to read the markings on a syringe and how units convert to mL on each syringe type. It is maths and label-reading, nothing else. It does not describe injection technique of any kind (no needle placement, no skin, and no steps of giving an injection), nor does it recommend any dose or make any health or benefit claims.

What U-100, U-50 and U-40 Mean

The number is simply how many unit marks fit in 1 mL on that syringe type.

U-40 40 units = 1 mL 1 unit = 0.025 mL Common on veterinary syringes.

The Liquid Does Not Change — Only the Ruler Changes

The liquid does not change between syringes — only the ruler printed on the barrel changes. A volume of 0.1 mL is the exact same amount of liquid in any syringe, but the tick marks on the barrel count it differently.

Section 2

Reading the Lines

How numbers, tick marks, and syringe sizes work together.

The numbers printed along the barrel show the big steps (on a 1 mL U-100 syringe the long lines and numbers usually mark every 10 units: 10, 20, 30 ... 100). The small lines between numbers each count 1 unit on most U-100 syringes. Always read from the 0 line at the top, and always check the type printed on the barrel before counting anything.

Common U-100 Sizes: A "Full Syringe" Is NOT Always 100 Units

Common U-100 syringe sizes: 0.3 mL (30 units total), 0.5 mL (50 units) and 1 mL (100 units) — the full count of a small syringe is a small number, so a "full syringe" is NOT always 100 units.

Section 3

The Same Dose on Three Syringes

A worked example showing how the same liquid reads on three scales.

Take the example mix from our units guide: a 5 mg vial mixed with 2 mL of water = 25 mcg per U-100 unit. An example dose of 250 mcg = 250 ÷ 25 = 10 units on a U-100 syringe — and 10 U-100 units is 10 × 0.01 mL = 0.1 mL of liquid.

Now the SAME 0.1 mL of the same liquid:

On a U-100 Syringe

0.1 mL × 100 = 10 units

10 units of 0.01 mL = 0.1 mL liquid.

U-50

On a U-50 Syringe

0.1 mL × 50 = 5 units

On a U-50 syringe it reads 0.1 × 50 = 5 units.

U-40

On a U-40 Syringe

0.1 mL × 40 = 4 units

On a U-40 syringe it reads 0.1 × 40 = 4 units.

Same dose, same liquid — three different numbers, because the rulers differ.

Section 4

The 2.5× Mistake

Why confusing syringe scales multiplies your volume.

Explain the Danger in Plain Maths

If someone works out "10 units" using U-100 maths but draws it on a U-40 syringe, those 10 U-40 units are 10 × 0.025 mL = 0.25 mL — two and a half times the liquid they meant (0.1 mL).

That is why the syringe type setting exists in the calculator, and why you should never copy unit numbers from someone using a different syringe.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Short, direct answers to common syringe-reading questions.

Why does the calculator ask for my syringe type?

Because the type changes the maths: the same liquid amount is 10 units on a U-100, 5 on a U-50 and 4 on a U-40. Wrong type in, wrong units out.

Is 10 units always 0.1 mL?

Only on a U-100 syringe. On a U-50, 10 units = 0.2 mL; on a U-40, 10 units = 0.25 mL.

My syringe is full at 30 units. Is it broken?

No — it is a 0.3 mL U-100 syringe. "Full" means its own maximum, not 100 units.

Can I use a U-40 syringe with this site's calculator?

Yes — select U-40 as the syringe type and the calculator converts for that scale. Never use U-100 numbers on a U-40 syringe.
