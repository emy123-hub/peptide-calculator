Vial Specifications

Common BPC-157 Vial Sizes

Understanding vial milligram content and liquid concentration.

BPC-157 is commonly sold in lyophilized powder form in 5 mg and 10 mg vials. Because the lyophilized powder occupies negligible volume when dissolved, the amount of bacteriostatic (BAC) water you add directly determines the final concentration of the solution.

The amount of BAC water you add changes the concentration, and the calculator shows the exact units for any combination. Adding more BAC water dilutes the solution, making each syringe unit contain fewer micrograms and easier to measure accurately on fine syringe markings. Conversely, using less water creates a more concentrated solution requiring fewer units to draw.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

BPC-157 reconstitution math, measurements, and stability facts.

How do I calculate BPC-157 units?

Calculating your syringe units follows four straightforward steps:

  1. Calculate solution concentration: Divide vial size (mg) by BAC water volume (mL). For example, 5 mg ÷ 2 mL = 2.5 mg/mL (2,500 mcg/mL).
  2. Convert dose unit: Ensure your desired dose is in matching units. If your dose is in mcg, divide by 1,000 to get mg (e.g., 250 mcg ÷ 1,000 = 0.25 mg).
  3. Find draw volume (mL): Divide your dose by the concentration. 0.25 mg ÷ 2.5 mg/mL = 0.10 mL.
  4. Convert volume to syringe units: Multiply the volume by your syringe calibration (for standard U-100 syringes with 100 units/mL: 0.10 mL × 100 = 10 units).
How much BAC water should I add to a 5 mg BPC-157 vial?

Adding more water makes each unit weaker and easier to measure; the right amount is the one where your dose is easy to draw without being too small or exceeding syringe limits:

  • With 1 mL BAC water: The solution concentration is 5 mg/mL (50 mcg per U-100 unit). A 250 mcg dose is only 5 units (0.05 mL), which can be difficult to measure precisely on a large syringe.
  • With 2 mL BAC water: The solution concentration is 2.5 mg/mL (25 mcg per U-100 unit). The same 250 mcg dose requires 10 units (0.10 mL), providing more tick marks for an easier and more forgiving draw.

You can use the calculator above to compare 1 mL vs. 2 mL with your own dose to see which draw volume is easier to measure on your syringe. There is no universally mandated dose or water volume.

How long does a mixed BPC-157 vial last?

Once reconstituted with bacteriostatic water, a mixed BPC-157 vial is generally used within 28–30 days when kept refrigerated at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C). The calculator shows your discard date in the optional protocol tracking section so you know exactly when the 28-day threshold is reached.

Is 250 mcg the same as 0.25 mg?

Yes — 1 mg = 1,000 mcg (pure metric conversion). To convert micrograms (mcg) to milligrams (mg), divide by 1,000 (250 mcg ÷ 1,000 = 0.25 mg). To convert milligrams to micrograms, multiply by 1,000 (0.25 mg × 1,000 = 250 mcg).
